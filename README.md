# Immersive Quest Markers

A "PDA-UI" style quest marker for STALKER GAMMA. Over the head of an NPC tied to one of your active tasks, it floats a small card (a header like `REPORT BACK` or `LOOKING FOR GUIDE`, plus the NPC's name) connected to the head by a thin diagonal **leader line**, the way TV and film UIs annotate a person or a phone on screen.

## Requirements
- MCM (optional) for in-game tuning; without it the built-in defaults apply.
- `demonized_geometry_ray` (optional) for line-of-sight culling. It ships with many GAMMA mods (both Catspaw addons, Weapon Cover Tilt, etc.), so it's effectively always present in GAMMA. Without it the LOS check is skipped and cards show through walls.
- No hard dependency: the marker is self-contained (own projection, own textures, own role glyphs); the utilities above only add optional polish.

## Upgrading
`gamedata/scripts/iqm_markers.script` was renamed to `iqm_core.script`. If you install by copying `gamedata` over an existing install, **delete the old `gamedata/scripts/iqm_markers.script`** — copying does not remove it, and with both files present the engine loads both, so every callback is registered twice and the mod runs its whole per-frame path twice over. MO2 users are unaffected: the mod is a self-contained folder that gets replaced wholesale.

## How it works
- **Projection is done by this mod**, with `game.world2ui` on the NPC's `bip01_head` bone. The node, leader line and card are all drawn from that one point, so they always agree.
- **Node picks the near shoulder:** so the leader line doesn't cross the torso, the anchor is placed on whichever shoulder sits on the card's side of the screen. Both shoulder candidates are projected to screen and the one toward the card is chosen, so it's correct at any camera angle and NPC facing. Auto by default; side and distance are tunable in MCM.
- **Smoothing:** the head point is eased with a framerate-independent exponential filter plus whole-pixel hysteresis, so the card sits steady while the NPC's head bobs. Big jumps snap.
- **Diagonal line:** the leader is a thin rotated strip from the head node to the card's nearest corner, extended slightly past the corner and drawn behind the plate so it tucks under the card with no gap. A slightly thicker black strip behind it keeps the line legible over bright backgrounds.
- The card flips to the other side of the head automatically near the screen edge, and fades in/out with distance.
- **Hidden while the PDA is open:** the whole overlay is suppressed when the fullscreen PDA (map / tasks / contacts) is up. Per-NPC state is kept, so closing the PDA resumes cleanly with no re-chirp or replayed entrance.
- **Line-of-sight culling:** a geometry ray is cast from the NPC's head toward the camera (via `demonized_geometry_ray`); if the head is behind a wall or other cover the card hides. It's polled per-NPC at a configurable rate with a short grace window so brief occlusions don't flicker. Without that utility the check is skipped and cards show whenever on-screen. Toggle in MCM.
- Up to 8 NPCs are carded at once; beyond that, extras are skipped for that scan (and noted in the log when diagnostic logging is on). Objective cards claim slots first, ambient role cards fill what's left.

## Focus mode
*Off by default.* Cards can follow your **gaze instead of your view** — only the person you're actually looking at is named, the way Red Dead Redemption 2 does it. It's the answer for a busy hub that isn't "turn the roles off".

- **The mode picks how much of the overlay is conditional**, in the order you'd want to give up: **Ambient only** focuses the service, VIP and `HIRING` cards and leaves every state card (objective, guide job, recruit, for hire) always visible — the "objectives always" rule above. **All but objective** also focuses the three state cards for people you happen to walk past. **Everything** includes the turn-in, for a bare screen.
- **It's a circle on the screen, not a box in the world.** The radius is a percentage of half the screen height — the same scale the waypoint markers' centre falloff uses, so 50 here is the same circle as 50 there — and it means the same thing at every resolution and aspect. Aiming down sights narrows what it covers in the world rather than in pixels — the view is magnified into the same ring — so scoping a distant figure names that figure and nothing beside them.
- **It's the opposite of what the markers do with the same circle, on purpose.** A marker *fades* near the crosshair, to get out of the way of what you're looking at; a card *appears* there, because it's what you're asking about. Run both and they cooperate: look at a stalker and their nameplate comes up while any badge sitting over them stands aside.
- **A soft edge, not a switch.** Cards ease in and out over the outer part of the radius (tunable, and 0 gives a hard boundary). They ease at the same rate as the distance fade, which is quicker than a head turn crosses the ring, so nothing lags behind where you're looking.
- **Unfocused doesn't have to mean gone.** The floor is 0 by default — outside the ring a card is simply not there — but raising it turns the feature into "quieten the rest" instead of "hide the rest".
- **It frees the slot, not just the pixels.** A card you aren't looking at gives up its place among the 8 to one you are, so a crowd can't spend every slot on people behind you and leave the person you turned to face unnamed. The rank is rebuilt early when you cross the ring rather than waiting out the scan interval, but only when the slots are actually contended.
- **The waypoint markers ignore it, deliberately.** A marker is for the NPC you *can't* see, so gating it on looking at them would leave it drawing only when it isn't needed. And looking away from a card doesn't hand you a marker in its place: an unfocused card still stands its marker down, so you get nothing, which is the point.
- The PDA chirp waits until the card is actually in focus, so you're never beeped at for a card you can't see.

## Card types
Headers follow two grammars on purpose. State cards use verb phrases: they mark something to act on right now, and they chirp on sighting. Ambient cards use a terse noun plus a role glyph: they state a standing fact about the NPC, and they stay silent. So the grammar alone tells you whether you are looking at an objective or at background information.

State cards (headers are written as the NPC broadcasting their own status on their PDA):
- **Quest objective**: the NPC the game is currently pointing you at. That covers a dynamic task (fetch/bounty) that has reached its hand-in stage, a storyline or mid-quest "go talk to X" step, and a delivery quest's "deliver to" NPC. Delivery targets are resolved directly from the delivery job's target functor (`task_functor.general_delivery`, ships with GAMMA), which the engine's own `current_target` can miss; they appear once you've travelled to that NPC's level and they're on-screen. All read `REPORT BACK`. Hostiles are never carded.
- **"Needs a guide" stalker**: the stalker currently looking for a guide to escort them somewhere (the GAMMA guide job). The guide squad is read from the job's own saved state, so the card matches exactly when that job is available, and it disappears the moment you accept the escort. Header reads `LOOKING FOR GUIDE`. Soft dependency on `tasks_guide` (ships with GAMMA); skipped if absent.
- **Recruitable companions**: nearby friendly stalkers you could recruit as a companion right now, for free. Header reads `LOOKING FOR WORK`. This scan is kept cheap: it skips entirely when your party is full, checks the cheapest conditions first per NPC, only keeps stalkers that project on-screen, and runs on a slow 3-second cadence with the result cached between scans. Eligibility uses GAMMA's actual recruit-dialog preconditions. Soft dependency on the companion system (ships with GAMMA); skipped if absent.
- **Stalkers for hire**: the paid half of the same dialog — stalkers who'd take money to leave their squad and work as your escort. Header reads `FOR HIRE`. Deliberately a separate card from the free recruit, because it is a disjoint set of NPCs: the free offer wants relation *friend*, the paid one wants relation *neutral*, and the paid one is limited to the communities that sell escort duty (Loners, Freedom, Mercenaries, Bandits), off the blacklisted maps, and outside Warfare. Both the individually-recruitable (pull one member out of a squad) and the base whole-squad-via-commander variants are covered. Resolved by the same 3-second scan, from the game's own dialog preconditions — including the phrase-level `is_squad_competent` gate that produces "Sorry, we have other jobs already", which only veteran and advanced squads can pass. Expect this card to be rare. That gate latches its verdict (and the price) the first time it is asked, so the scan asks it last, only for a squad that has cleared every other check and is already on-screen beside you; the card then matches the dialogue exactly and clears for a squad that has refused you. With modded exes the card also carries the actual 24h price and hides when you cannot cover it — the multiplier and base prices are file locals, so this needs the unlocalizer in `configs/unlocalizers/iqm_unlocalizers.ltx`. Note the base game tests affordability against a hardcoded 2000 x multiplier while charging 1500 x multiplier, so you need 2700 RU on you to be offered a 2025 RU hire; the card mirrors the test and displays the charge.

Ambient role cards are detected from the engine's own PDA map-spot registry (the same source as the map legend icons), polled per NPC on a slow cadence:
- **Guides**: NPCs offering fast-travel guide services (the PDA "Guide" icon). Header reads `GUIDE`.
- **Traders / technicians / barkeeps / medics**: service NPCs. The exact service is read from the NPC's actual trade file via `trader_autoinject`, so a technician whose level logic labels him a trader still reads `TECHNICIAN`. Headers read `TRADER` / `TECHNICIAN` / `BARKEEP` / `MEDIC`.
- **Important characters**: story/faction important NPCs (the PDA "Important Character" and quest-NPC icons). Header reads `VIP`.
- **Task givers** *(experimental, off by default)*: any friendly stalker who would actually offer you a task if you asked. Header reads `HIRING`, plus the job kind when the offered task is classifiable: `HIRING: SUPPLIES` / `MUTANT HUNT` / `ASSAULT` / `BOUNTY` / `RESCUE` / `DELIVERY` / `ARTIFACT` / `ESCORT` / `MUTANT PARTS` / `GEAR` / `PATCHES` / `RECOVERY` / `FUNDS` / `SURVEY` / `TOOLS` (technician jobs) / `SPECIAL` (unique story jobs). The kind is read once per task id from the task section's `status_functor` (generic fetch jobs split further by their `fetch_func` item category). With faction icons on, hiring cards show the NPC's faction emblem, so the card reads as "Duty is hiring". Unlike the other roles (read from cheap engine registries), this queries the task system itself; the checks are cached and rationed as described below, but it's the newest and heaviest detector, which is why it carries the experimental label and ships disabled. The verdict comes from the same machinery the game itself answers with: the dialogue task cache where that works, or the PDA-taskboard generator on GAMMA (whose dialogue path is monkey-patched, so work flows through the board there). No card means "nothing at the moment". The card also hides while you already hold the per-NPC task limit. Detection is built around the cost of that lookup (it walks every task section in the game): answers come free from the task system's own per-NPC memory (fresh for 1.5 in-game hours) and from the PDA taskboard's pinned offers, and only NPCs with no recorded answer are actively checked, one at a time, nearest first, on an adaptive budget: each check is timed and the next is spaced at ~50× its cost (0.3 s floor, 3 s ceiling), so probing never takes more than about 2% of frame time, even on a weak CPU. A probe waits for a frame with no NPC scan slice running; once overdue it shares a frame rather than starve. Active checking does exactly what asking the NPC for work does (including rolling whether they have a task this cycle), just earlier; an MCM toggle (Advanced) turns it off for a purely passive detector. Full details in `iqm_taskwork.script`.
- Role cards default to the noun header and name with the role glyph beside them, tinted to the accent colour. The glyphs are bundled with the mod (Tabler icons). An MCM style option (Advanced) switches them to a compact icon-only chip (just the glyph on a small plate, for quieter hubs) or to plain text with no glyph.

When one NPC qualifies for several cards, the most actionable wins: objective > guide job > recruitable > hiring > role. A quest giver who is also a trader reads `REPORT BACK` while they're your objective, then goes back to the `TRADER` chip; a trader with a job on offer reads `HIRING` until you take it.

With `HIRING` in the mix a busy camp can produce more candidates than the 8 card slots; slots go to the highest-priority, closest NPCs (distance is banded so cards don't reshuffle as people wander).

## Waypoint markers
Cards only work when you can see the NPC. The waypoint marker covers the case they can't: a **role glyph plus a direction chevron**, drawn **through walls** and parked against the screen edge when the NPC is behind you, with the range in metres underneath.

**Out of the box it marks four things: the NPC an active task wants you to report back to, the waypoint you placed yourself, guide NPCs, and the service trades** (trader / technician / barkeep / medic) — each on its own switch on the Waypoint markers page, and the guides and the trades are there because a shop you are walking to across a hub is as much a destination as a turn-in is. What keeps a hub from filling with badges is the 60 m range, not the switches. A fifth extends it to **your own companions**, and that one is off by default. Nothing else can carry one, deliberately: a marker is for a place you are *going*, and the state roles (needs-a-guide, recruitable, for hire) are people you happen to walk past — marking those turned the screen into an objective list, which is the opposite of the point. Their cards still work exactly as before.

- **Your own waypoint gets one too**, if you have [Personal Adjustable Waypoint](https://www.moddb.com/mods/stalker-anomaly/addons/personal-adjustable-waypoint) (GAMMA does). Drop a waypoint on the map or by aiming at something, and it's marked through walls with the **same ring your PDA map draws around it** — at *any* range, unlike every other marker here. The distance limit below exists so the overlay doesn't hand you positions you haven't found; a waypoint is a position you chose. It steps aside once you're standing on it (3 m), and it takes a marker slot ahead of the NPCs, so a busy camp can't quietly drop the one mark you placed by hand. The ground route follows it as well — see *Route*.

- **Your companions can carry one, off by default.** This is the one mark here that is not a destination — a companion is usually behind you — and the exception is stated rather than smuggled in. What the rule above is actually about is having a specific reason to want to know where a body is at the moment geometry has taken it away from you. A recruitable stalker fails that: once you have walked past him you have no reason to care where he went. A companion passes it harder than anything else on the list, because it is your own asset and losing it behind a warehouse is the exact case the marker was built for. It wears the same bust your PDA map puts on a companion, rides the same distance as the guide and service markers, and is ranked **behind every role** — so a companion can never take a slot from a turn-in or a shop. It is the one mark here that is allowed to lose one. Needs the companion system (`axr_companions`, ships with GAMMA); without it the switch does not appear at all.

- **The mark is the one on your map.** Each glyph is rendered from the same source art as that role's PDA map spot: the diamond your map puts on a turn-in, the briefcase on a trader, the cross on a medic. What you learn on the map is what you look for through the wall. (Guides are the exception — vanilla doesn't map-spot them, so they keep their own glyph.)
- **A card and a waypoint marker are never both up on the same NPC.** The marker exists only for the case the card can't cover — beyond card range, behind cover, or off-screen — and is suppressed outright the moment the card is up, then dissolves back in when it goes down. It's a handover, not a blend: they never share the screen for an NPC beyond the fraction of a second the dissolve takes. Every reason a card hides brings the marker back, without re-testing any of them — **except focus mode**, which is the one reason that doesn't. A marker is for a body geometry or distance has taken away from you; focus hasn't taken it anywhere, it's still right there beside your crosshair, and handing you a through-wall badge for the stalker you just looked away from would undo the very thing you turned focus on for. So an unfocused card keeps its marker stood down and you get nothing, which is the point of it.
- **When there are more marks than slots, the quest wins.** At most six are on screen at once, and they're ranked the way the cards are — the waypoint you placed first, then task turn-ins, then the ambient trades and guides, and your companions last of all — with the nearest first *within* each of those. Before, they were ranked by distance alone, so in a busy hub a technician you happened to be standing nearer to could silently push a turn-in off the end; the turn-in then appeared only once you'd walked close enough to out-rank a shop, which read as a marker that flickered.
- **Deliberately short-ranged.** Default 60 m, and it never drops below the appear distance. This is an aid for finding someone once you're near them, not cross-map navigation: it should save you circling a camp, not turn the Zone into a waypoint chase. Raise it in MCM if you disagree.
- **Nothing behind it.** No plate, no panel: the glyph and the range sit straight on the world, and each carries a black keyline **baked into its texture** for contrast — line art with no contrast floor disappears over pale wood, and an outline buys the same legibility a plate did without putting a slab of UI over the scene. Baked rather than drawn as a second black widget behind, because a second widget cannot stay aligned with the first: the engine floors every widget's position to a whole screen pixel independently, so a separate outline shifts by a pixel as you walk and is uneven while you stand still. (The plate is still in the code behind one flag, `BADGE_PLATE`, if you want it back.)
- **The chevron only appears when the marker is parked at a screen edge.** There it is the only thing telling you where the NPC is, and it swings **outward** to point at them — including when they're behind you (the projection mirrors through the screen centre there, which the marker undoes; otherwise the arrow would point exactly the wrong way). While the marker is on screen it's already sitting over their head, so there's no arrow: one pointing down at the thing it's attached to said nothing and only added a second mark to read.
- **It gets out of the way of what you're looking at.** A marker near the centre of the screen shrinks and fades, because the thing under your crosshair is the thing you want to see — so a stalker behind a wall dead ahead stands down while you deal with whatever is actually in front of you, and comes back up as you turn off it. It fades to a floor rather than away, so nothing pops in and out as you pan, and the shrink is much shallower than the fade: opacity is what does the getting out of the way. Radius and floor are on the Waypoint markers page under Advanced, and 0 turns it off. **Only the fade is on by default**: the marker holds its size and just goes quiet, because a mark that changes size is a mark whose position your eye has to find again. Switch the shrink back on if you'd rather it visibly stood down as well. The fade isn't optional either way; it's the half that actually clears the view.  
  Two things it deliberately does *not* touch. A marker parked at a screen edge, because one sitting against the edge of your view was never in the way of the middle of it. And **the waypoint you placed yourself**, at any distance from the centre — it is the one mark here that is *meant* to sit dead ahead for minutes at a time, because it is the thing you are walking toward, and fading it exactly when it is confirming you are on course is the opposite of helpful. Your objective marker does fade: it never disappears, and the route on the ground is already telling you the same thing.
- **Waypoint an NPC the mod is already marking and the two marks merge.** Drop your waypoint on the medic you're heading to and you don't get two badges on one body — the medic's marker wears the waypoint's own ring, the same four-arc pulse your PDA draws around a placed waypoint. Beyond marker range the waypoint's own marker takes over as before, so there's never a gap where neither shows.
- **The NPC's name can go above it** (Advanced, off by default). Worth one caveat before you turn it on: it's the only part of the marker that *can't* follow the size slider. Everything else is the mod's own artwork and scales with it; a name has to be real text, and the engine draws text at one of a few fixed sizes picked from your screen resolution. So a small marker gets a name that towers over it and a large one gets a caption. Near the default size they agree. Your own placed waypoint gets no name — there's no NPC to name.
- **The range readout is drawn, not typed.** It comes from the mod's own digit sheet (Bahnschrift/DIN, condensed) rather than a game font. That isn't cosmetic: an engine font renders at one of eleven fixed sizes chosen by a resolution bucket and cannot be scaled per widget, so a text readout could neither be small enough nor follow the marker size. As sprites it's a percentage of the marker (Advanced, default 40%), and it scales with it.
- **By default the colour is the one on your map too** (*Waypoint marker colour*, shipped on **Map icon + faction turn-ins**; set it to *Accent* for the mod's own gold on everything). **Map icon** gives each marker the colour its PDA spot carries — **turn-ins the hand-in green**, trader gold, barkeep orange, technician steel blue, medic coral. **Map icon + faction turn-ins** is the same, except a turn-in takes the quest giver's faction colour, with traders and anyone else who has no faction of their own taking the loner amber. Guides and your own waypoint always keep the accent: the game doesn't map-spot a guide at all, and the waypoint's map colour is a pure blue that wouldn't read over the world.

  Markers took the accent and nothing else until now, because they're read at a glance, often against sky, and a dark tint would make them the least legible thing on screen. That's much less true since each mark started carrying its own baked keyline (above) — an outline is what lets a dark colour keep its edge against sky and foliage. It's why the accent was the only option for so long, and why it's still one list entry away for anyone who'd rather have the overlay in a single voice.
- **Or a colour of the marker's own** (*Colour → Custom*, with red/green/blue in Advanced). The fourth setting on that same list puts every marker on one colour you pick, instead of the nameplates' accent — a badge found against sky at 200 m and a plate read while you're standing in front of somebody are not the same object, which is why the route and the minimap trail have always had their own colours too. The three channels start at the accent's own gold, so picking Custom changes nothing until you move one: it's a starting position, not a jump to some other colour. Turn-in green, trader gold and the rest belong to *Map icon*; the mutant hunt's lime and the bounty's red are the task kind speaking and are unaffected either way. The head node and leader line on the nameplate stay on the accent, so a colour far from the gold is deliberately two colours at two distances.
- Up to 4 NPCs are marked at once, nearest first, so a near one you can already see can't crowd out a far one you can't.
- Hidden with everything else while the PDA is open, the HUD is down, or the reveal hotkey has the overlay off.
- Needs `game.world2ui_with_depth` (modded exes, ships with GAMMA) for the behind-camera test; on a bare Anomaly exe the markers disable themselves and the cards are unaffected.
- The F7 / Lua pin actions work on markers too: `iqm_debug.debug_card("trader")` pins the role, then walk away or turn around and the marker for it appears (with the service-NPC switch on).

## Route
*Off by default; switch it on under Route: ground.* A beacon tells you *where* someone is. It doesn't tell you how to get there, and in the Zone those are different questions — the building between you and them has a way round it, and the beacon points straight through it. So the objective target also gets a **route painted on the ground**: a continuous line following walkable terrain, thicker near you and thinner as it recedes, so it converges the way a painted line should.

**It reads as paint on the floor.** A run of **amber marks**, laid out ahead of you every few metres and flowing with you as you walk. They foreshorten with the ground they sit on, and they're finished like paint rather than like a decal: the surface is worn and thin in places, the outline is a brighter lip around a slightly sunk body, and each mark casts a soft contact shadow that sits it on the floor instead of over it.

**The head of the route comes to you.** Step sideways off the line and its near end follows, bending back onto the walkable path over the next few metres — so the marks in front of you keep facing you instead of sliding round as you strafe.

**Three mark shapes, in MCM.** All of them point: an **arrowhead**, and two **chevrons** at different sizes. There is a real trade between them. A mark on the ground is drawn as one flat quad, and the more depth a shape spans along the direction of travel, the less exactly that quad can match the ground under it, which shows as a slight shear that shifts as you strafe. The arrowhead is the widest and lies flattest; the small chevron has the hardest point and pays for it. Flatter shapes that carried no direction at all were tried and dropped — a mark you can't read a heading off is decoration. Spacing, opacity and colour are in MCM too.

Spaced marks came first, and were tried at 4 m and then at 2 m before being dropped. A row of separate glyphs says "here are some points on the way" and leaves the joining to your eye, which at a distance over broken ground it doesn't do — and an axis of advance is drawn with a continuous line anyway.

- **It knows about walls without hiding behind them.** Stretches that are actually out of sight fade down rather than disappearing, so you keep the depth cue and still get the route round the corner — hiding the occluded part would blank exactly the bit that answers the question. Each stretch fades on its own and eases into it, so nothing pops. Turn it off in MCM if you'd rather the route stopped dead at the corner.
- **It steps aside once you can see them, but only up close.** Within 25 m, seeing the target puts the route away — the marker over their head says the rest, and a line to the feet of someone in front of you is clutter. Further out it stays: being able to make out a figure across a field tells you nothing about how to walk there. Either way it's put away rather than thrown away, so stepping back behind cover brings it straight back with no recompute, and a glimpse through a doorway costs nothing.
- **It starts at your feet.** The first stretch is clipped to the near plane rather than dropped, so the line begins under you instead of a few metres out and stepping forward as you walk.
- **It's drawn out from you, not in from the target.** The route distance says how much of the way you can see, not how near the target has to be — a quest giver right across the level still gets you the next 20 m of ground. It never tells you where they are, only which way the ground goes, and it runs out long before you get there, so it's a weaker aid than the map marker the game already gives you. It stops only when you're basically standing on them.
- **Deliberately short-ranged, like the beacon.** Default 20 m — the way round the building in front of you, not a line laid most of the way to somewhere you haven't found — and that same number caps how much path is drawn at once — measured along the path, so a route doubling back round a building is capped by how far you'd *walk*, not how far away the target is standing.
- **It bends where the ground bends.** The line's corners are put where the path actually turns rather than at even intervals, so it goes through a doorway instead of clipping the jamb — and a long straight costs a handful of segments rather than one per metre.
- **Walking it is free.** The route isn't recomputed as you follow it; it's trimmed behind you. A fresh search happens only when the target moves off, when you stray off the line, or when the objective changes — and the search itself is spread across frames rather than run in one go, so a long route can't land as a hitch.
- The path follows the **AI navmesh**, so it goes where NPCs can walk. It won't use ladders or jumps, and in a few spots it'll take a route you wouldn't. If it can't find the whole way — the target's on a roof, or inside geometry the mesh doesn't cover, or simply a long way off — you get the route as far as it got rather than nothing, and the beacon still marks where they actually are.
- **It can be summoned rather than standing.** Hold the key and the line draws; let go and it holds for three seconds, then fades. A tap is enough, which is the point: you glance at the route rather than holding a button through a firefight. It's the strongest version of this mod's own rule that a route shouldn't turn the Zone into a waypoint chase — a line that only exists in the seconds you asked for it can't be followed mindlessly. Cheap, too: the route is blanked rather than thrown away, so summoning it never costs a search. It isn't the default, because the route itself is off until you switch it on and a feature you just enabled should draw something: out of the box the line stands, and the summon (or *follow the nameplates*) is one list entry away in MCM.
- Bind the summon to **its own key** if you'd rather keep nameplates up and pulse the route on its own; leave it unbound and it borrows the nameplate hotkey, where a tap will also toggle the nameplates. With no key bound anywhere the route is simply drawn all the time, so a half-configured menu can't leave you with no route and no way to ask for one.
- Same gating as everything else: hidden while the PDA is open or the HUD is down. Blanking keeps the route, so bringing it back is instant.
- One route at a time, and only for the objective target. A path swinging between a quest giver and a mechanic as you cross a hub would read as a bug rather than a feature.
- Needs `game.world2ui_with_depth` (modded exes, ships with GAMMA) to project the markers, and `ray_pick` to know what's behind a wall. Without either the route disables itself and everything else is unaffected — the same arrangement the beacon uses, and it shares the projection check with it.

## Minimap trail
The same route, shown a second way: a line of small marks on the HUD minimap. Off by default.

- **It follows the task you selected.** Pick a different task in your PDA and both the ground line and the minimap trail follow it — they're two views of one route, so they always agree about where you're going.
- **It's independent of the ground line.** Have either, both, or neither. It's also the only one of the two that works on a setup that can't draw the world-space route at all, since it never projects anything.
- **It starts under your feet and ends on the target.** The trail begins at the player arrow rather than a few metres out, and always puts a mark on the objective when the objective is on the map — so you never have to guess which of the last few marks meant "here".
- **It knows how big your minimap is, whatever your HUD.** Half a dozen mods ship minimap layouts and the file the game picks depends on your aspect ratio, so the mod measures the map rather than assuming it: it reads back where the engine actually draws a marker and works out the scale from that. It re-measures on its own after a level change or a resolution change.
- **It stands down when you're nearly there.** Inside 10 m the trail is three marks under the player arrow telling you nothing, so it hides — earlier than the ground line, which is still worth walking along at that range.
- **Its own colour.** Three sliders on the minimap page, white by default. Separate from the ground route's colour on purpose: that one is chosen to sit in the scene you're walking through, while this is read off a map against terrain, your own arrow and everyone else's spots. Each mark keeps its black outline whatever you pick, because the outline is part of the artwork rather than a tint. No opacity slider to go with them — the marks are solid, and they're culled before the map's edge, so there's no boundary for a fade to soften.
- Marks are drawn over the map rather than under it, and are culled at the edge instead of piling up on the rim.
- Needs the modded exes' DXML support (ships with GAMMA) for its one-pixel calibration marker. Without it the trail simply never appears and nothing else is affected.

## PDA map icons
The mod also redraws the markers the *engine* puts on your PDA map and HUD minimap — tasks, the four trades, VIPs, companions, level transitions and the rest — from its own 128 px art at the tints the overlay uses, so the map and the world agree about what a mark means.

- **Nothing is overwritten.** The spot definitions are patched in memory through DXML, not by shipping a copy of `map_spots*.xml`: several UI mods ship that file, and a copy of it would silently revert whichever of them won. The script's filename places it in a load-order window — after the mods that re-point the same elements, before the one that corrects them for your aspect ratio.
- **The Symbols panel is updated to match.** The legend's rows are found by the art they already carry, which keeps it self-limiting: a legend layout we don't recognise is left alone, and so is any row whose marker we don't redraw (stashes, Area, fast travel, the guide, the player arrow and the relation dots all keep their own icons). Vanilla's rows also have a hard-coded blue on them, which is removed so each swatch takes its marker's real colour.
- **The NPC pins carry a name.** Hovering a trader, technician, bartender, medic, guide or important character on the PDA map gives you *Sidorovich - Trader* rather than *Trader*, so a hub full of identical pins can be read without walking to each one — and *Wolf - Important Character* says something, where the pin on its own does not. It rewrites the tooltip the game already sets and changes nothing about which NPCs get a pin; switching it off in MCM puts the plain wording straight back, on the pins already drawn.
- **Your companions stop drawing a squad dot**, on by default. A companion's squad is still a squad as far as the simulation is concerned, so the map and the minimap put the ordinary faction dot on them — trailing your own arrow by a step, answering where the person walking beside you is. It comes off; every other squad in the Zone keeps its dot, and which of those you see at all is still your PDA's business. This is the one thing here that removes a marker rather than redrawing one, so it is the one thing here with a switch that means *absent*. Switching it off puts the dots straight back, without waiting for anything.
- **A STALKER 2 look, off by default.** *Icon style* in MCM swaps the ring badges for the way STALKER 2 draws a world marker: a white glyph in a thin white diamond, with no colour coding at all. Same glyph set — it's the frame and the tint that change — so a service is told apart by its glyph alone, and the frame stops saying whether a mark is a person standing there, a place to go and search or a job to hand in. The squad dots keep their faction colours either way, since there the colour *is* the information, and so does the level transition arch — it's the same arch in both styles, so bleaching it would only have made a fixture harder to find. The diamonds are drawn slightly larger than the badges they replace, because a diamond of the same width reads smaller than a ring. The trader is the one glyph that differs between the two: the diamond gives its glyph more room than a ring does, so it draws a bag rather than the briefcase. Read at startup like the row above it, so it applies at the next launch.
- Without the modded exes' DXML support the whole thing simply never runs and the game's own icons stay.

## Interference
During an emission and inside a psi zone the overlay stops holding still. **On by default** — it's the point rather than a garnish: a mark that the Zone can interfere with is a mark something is *rendering*, and one that's always perfect is a menu drawn over the game.

- **It builds and fades with the emission itself.** The pressure follows the blowout's own stages — the drop before you hear anything, the impact, the two waves, the long decay — so the overlay peaks when the sky does rather than running its own schedule.
- **It arrives in stages, not all at once.** First the marks tear sideways in bands. Then their outlines separate into a coloured fringe and the range readout starts printing a wrong digit. Then the whole overlay drops out for a beat at a time. Each step keeps the ones before it, so what you see is something degrading rather than something wobbling harder.
- **Get under cover and it eases off** — not to nothing, because the emission is still happening, but enough that you can read the marks again.
- **A psi zone does the same thing, and so does a hit.** Walk into a psi field, get a controller's attention, stand near a storm vortex, and the overlay takes the knock and then recovers on its own. It settles as soon as you're clear.
- **It never quietly hides an objective.** At the very worst of it the marks fade to just-visible rather than disappearing, because a marker that's gone looks exactly like a marker that failed. If you'd rather it cut out completely there's a switch for that, off by default.
- **Two knobs and a switch**, on the General page: on/off, strength (10–200%), and whether to allow the full cut-out. The minimap trail is deliberately left out of all of it — it's a map, not a thing in the world.

## MCM (Options → Mod Configuration → Immersive Quest Markers)
Split across two tabs, each opened by the mod banner and then broken into captioned sections. **Core** holds the everyday switches; **Advanced** holds fine tuning, styling, colour, motion and performance rates. A section whose every option is hidden (the beacon groups, when the card role behind them is off) takes its caption with it, so the page never shows a header over nothing.

**Core** — `Enable markers` sits on its own above the first section.
- **Card roles:** quest targets · the "needs a guide" stalker · recruitable companions · stalkers you can hire · guide NPCs · service NPCs (trader/technician/barkeep/medic) · important characters · NPCs with work available (experimental).
- **Factions:** faction colours, faction emblems.
- **Visibility:** appear distance, line-of-sight check.
- **Hand-in beacon:** beacon the hand-in target, beacon distance. Hidden entirely unless quest targets are being carded.
- **Route:** draw the ground route to the selected task's marker, when it's drawn (summoned / follow nameplates / always), how long it holds after you let go, its own summon key and modifier, how far ahead to draw it, and whether to show the same route as a trail on the minimap. Both views are off by default — the route is the one aid here that answers *how do I get there*, so it's opted into rather than handed to you — and either works without the other. Hidden entirely unless quest targets are being carded.
- **Alerts:** PDA chirp on sighting.
- **Reveal hotkey:** key, modifier, activation mode. With no key bound the cards show whenever they qualify, as before. Bind one to gate them instead, in any of MCM's standard activation modes — press, double-tap or long press to toggle them on and off — plus a fourth, *hold to show*: cards only while the key is physically down, so the world stays clean until you ask. An optional shift/ctrl/alt modifier is honoured, and the long-press time and double-tap window come from MCM's own Key Binds settings, so they match every other mod that uses them. Detection keeps running while the cards are hidden, so bringing them back is instant — no rescan, no repeated chirp, no replayed entrance. Needs MCM 1.6.0 or newer.

**Advanced**
- **Card:** service card style (icon chip / text + icon / text only), abbreviate NPC ranks, card offset X/Y, card opacity.
- **Node and line:** show head node dot, pulse the node glow, node size, node min size (far), leader line thickness and opacity.
- **Anchor:** height above the shoulder, horizontal anchor nudge, auto shoulder side + distance.
- **Motion:** smoothing (framerate-independent), snap distance.
- **Accent colour:** A/R/G/B for the line, edge bar, header and node. Defaults to a soft desaturated gold (224, 196, 122) on a warm charcoal plate.
- **Beacon roles:** the needs-a-guide stalker · recruitable companions · stalkers for hire · guide NPCs · service NPCs · important characters · NPCs with work. All off by default. Turning one on for a role whose card you've switched off does nothing until you switch the card back on — the switch is still there to read, rather than vanishing until you've been out of the menu and back in. Your own companions sit here too: there is no companion card, so that switch appears whenever GAMMA's companion system is installed and not at all without it.
- **Beacon appearance:** range readout on/off · beacon size (one knob: the badge, arrow and spacing all scale from it) · screen-edge inset · beacon opacity.
- **Route appearance:** route style — chevrons only (bold one-piece chevrons lying flat on the ground with no line under them, the default), an arrow band (a narrow line with chevrons along it), arrows only, line + widely spaced markers, or the arrow band with one-piece chevrons · line width in centimetres of ground (the line is a band lying on the floor, so how thick it looks is a consequence of where you stand) · chevron size · chevron spacing (3 m by default, which is close enough that the chevrons read as one band) · route opacity · route colour R/G/B · show the route through walls. The route carries its own colour rather than the accent above — it's scenery you walk along, not a marker you glance at — and defaults to amber (232, 196, 92), which is what floor markings actually are. **If you've already set a route colour, MCM keeps it** and you'll want to change it by hand to see the new default.
- **Minimap trail:** mark size · arrows or plain beads. Shown only when the trail is switched on. Chevron spacing, drawn distance and the through-walls fade sit with the route options above, since they shape the route both views share.
- **Fade and line of sight:** full-opacity distance, line-of-sight check rate, line-of-sight grace (anti-flicker linger).
- **Sound:** PDA chirp volume.
- **Work detection:** actively check for work (turn off for a purely passive task-giver detector).
- **Diagnostics:** diagnostic logging (line-of-sight checks, card-slot overflow, sound playback). Off by default; the mod writes nothing to the log without it.

Why each default is the value it is — what it was before, what was tried against it, and the measurements that settled it — is recorded in `docs/decisions.md`, one entry per option key. The option registry in `iqm_core.script` keeps the one-line description and points at it.

## Testing without quests
A card can be forced onto the stalker under your crosshair, bypassing the task/guide/companion detection. Two ways:

- **F7 debug menu → Execute tab**: look at a stalker, press F7, and use the injected `IQM: Card as Quest Target / Guide / Companion / Paid Escort / Guide NPC / Trader / Important / Has-Work` actions (plus `IQM: Cards Clear`, and `IQM: Cards Task-Work Dump` to print the work detector's cache/queue state). The menu closes and the card appears immediately. `Card as Trader` cycles a random service glyph (trader / technician / barkeep / medic) each press, so all four can be previewed from one button.
- **Lua execute box / script console**:

  ```lua
  iqm_debug.debug_card("target")       -- or "guide" / "companion" / "hire" / "guider" / "trader" /
                                       -- "mechanic" / "barman" / "medic" / "important" / "work"
  ```

Repeating an action with the same role unpins that NPC; a different role switches the card style. `iqm_debug.debug_clear()` removes all pins. Pinned cards persist until unpinned (or the NPC dies), so you can test fade distances, line-of-sight culling, the shoulder picker and the chirp on any friendly NPC.

The route can be exercised the same way. **F7 → Execute** injects:

- `IQM: Route Status` — dumps the live driver's state, including a plain-English reason when there's nothing on screen, so "why is there no route" is one keypress rather than a guess. The most useful of these by a distance.
- `IQM: Route To Aim Point` / `IQM: Route Clear` — route from your feet to whatever you're aiming at and draw it, through the same budgeted search the shipping route uses.
- `IQM: Raw Path vs Ours` — draws the bare A\* answer in red beside the finished route in green, through one renderer with identical options, so "the search is wrong" and "we mangled a good answer" stop being the same observation.
- `IQM: Path Hide` / `IQM: Path Clear`, and `IQM: Navmesh Snapshot` (BFS hop-distance colouring of the navmesh around you).

While a driver holds the ribbon the live route parks itself, and hands it back when you clear it.

> The tuning cyclers that used to sit here — spacing, length, colour, flow, heading baseline, x-ray, and the path style/cull/trail toggles — were retired at R2.44. Every one wrote a value the next `read_config` would silently overwrite, which is a confusing state to leave a tester in, and the questions they existed to answer are recorded as closed in `docs/ar-navigation.md`. Use the MCM pages instead; what those cyclers actually guarded is now asserted through `configure()` in `tools/nav-harness`.

**`python tools/check-lua.py`** is the one command to run before shipping anything. It compiles every script the way the engine does — with the namespace header the engine prepends, under LuaJIT 2.0, the same generation the game embeds (Anomaly runs LuaJIT 2.0.4) — and then runs every harness under that same Lua. It also prints how much of each file's 198-local budget is left, which matters more than it sounds: a `.script` body is one Lua function, Lua 5.1 caps a function at 200 locals, and going over is a *load-time syntax error* that takes the whole module down rather than anything you could debug in game. Needs `pip install lupa`, which ships a prebuilt LuaJIT 2.0 — nothing to compile and no Lua needed on PATH. The compile-and-budget half lives in the `gamma-anomaly-debug` skill (`scripts/check_lua.py`) rather than here, since none of it is specific to this mod; set `IQM_GAMMA_SKILL` if the skill lives somewhere unusual.

The harnesses it runs cover the parts that are miserable to judge in game: the route harness runs the real A* over a synthetic navmesh with a wall and a 1 m doorway, the pathline harness covers line geometry, chevrons, gizmo pooling and occlusion culling, the nav harness drives the route policy over a fake clock — when a search is allowed to start, what walking costs, whether losing and regaining sight of someone behaves, and where the stroke puts its vertices — and the taskwork harness dumps the work detector's state. Each can still be run on its own with any Lua 5.1; expected results are in each harness header.

The work detector also has an offline test harness that runs without launching the game: running `tools/taskwork-harness/harness.lua` under any Lua 5.1 (LuaJIT included) with `vanilla` or `gamma` as its argument loads the real `iqm_taskwork.script` against a stubbed engine (including GAMMA's broken dialogue-cache monkey in `gamma` mode) and exercises the probe/cache/verdict pipeline end to end. Setting `SLOW_PROBE=1` simulates a slow CPU so you can watch the adaptive probe spacing react. Expected results are in the harness header.
